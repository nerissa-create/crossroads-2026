Julia Dale

CEO, Civilla

Julia Dale is the CEO of Civilla, a non-profit organization dedicated to transforming the way public-serving institutions work through human-centered design. She joined Civilla in 2025 after a 23-year career in public service within Michigan state government. Julia’s commitment to public service is deeply personal. Raised in poverty by a single mother, she benefited directly from public programs that helped set her on a path to thrive. These experiences shaped her life’s mission: to help others navigate difficult circumstances with dignity and support. As Civilla’s CEO, she leads the organization’s long-term strategy to help institutions across the country become more efficient, effective, and human.

Read more

Julia first partnered with Civilla in 2021 while serving as director of Michigan’s Unemployment Insurance Agency (UIA). Appointed during the height of the COVID-19 pandemic, she stepped into leadership at one of the most challenging times in the agency’s history. She led efforts to restore trust and improve services, including the development of a digital tool to support claimants and employers through the benefits process with the Civilla team. Now, as CEO, Julia leads Civilla’s long-term strategy to bring similar people-first improvements to institutions across the country.

Before leading UIA, Julia served as an Assistant Attorney General and Head of the Business and Charities Section in the Michigan Attorney General’s Office. She also held leadership roles at the Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs, including Cemetery Commissioner and Director of the Corporations Division. Julia began her public service career in 2001 as a Civil Rights Representative and later a Regional Office Manager for the Michigan Department of Civil Rights.

Julia earned her BA in Political Theory, Constitutional Democracy, and Social Relations from James Madison College at Michigan State University and her JD from Wayne State University Law School. She brings curiosity, courage, and compassion to her work and is passionate about igniting that same courage in other leaders.

Marina Nitze

Partner, Layer Aleph

Marina is a partner in the crisis engineering firm Layer Aleph and previously served as CTO of the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, where she helped found the U.S. Digital Service.

Read more

Marina (Martin) Nitze, co-author of the books Crisis Engineering (opens in a new tab) and Hack Your Bureaucracy (opens in a new tab), is currently focused on improving America’s child welfare system, while also helping organizations solve mission-critical IT challenges. She was previously the Chief Technology Officer of the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (2013–2017), helped found the United States Digital Service, and served as a Senior Advisor on technology in the White House in the Obama Administration.

She is a partner in the crisis engineering firm Layer Aleph, which restores complex systems to service and helps organizations emerge stronger from existential crises.

Marina currently works with states to surface and scale promising practices in foster care, in order to keep at-risk kids safe and safely reduce time spent in foster care, as a Fellow at the New Practice Lab at New America. She has been an active volunteer Court-Appointed Special Advocate for at-risk foster youth for over 20 years.

Marina serves on the board of directors at Renaissance Philanthropy and Ameelio, and on the advisory boards for the Center for Civic Futures, Foster Insights, Grandfamilies & Kinship Support Network, Recoding America, and Tech Viaduct.

Earlier in her career, Marina built the first agency United States Digital Service team at the VA, bringing in America’s top technical talent for tours of duty to help transform how America serves and honors its Veterans. Her portfolio included vets.gov (now VA.gov), Caseflow, and the Clinician User Interface. She was formerly Senior Advisor to U.S. CTO Todd Park, and served as the first Entrepreneur-in-Residence at the U.S. Department of Education as part of the inaugural class of Presidential Innovation Fellows.

Prior to joining the federal government, Marina was Founder & CEO of the business efficiency consulting firm The Type-A Way and author of the book Business Efficiency for Dummies. She has also been recognized as a Technology and Democracy Fellow at the Harvard Kennedy School. Marina lives in Seattle, Washington with her husband Charles and dog, Fiona.

Lynn Overmann

Executive Director, Beeck Center for Social Impact + Innovation, Georgetown University

Lynn leads the Beeck Center for Social Impact + Innovation at Georgetown University, following a career spanning policy, data, and government delivery — including senior roles with the U.S. Digital Service, USDA, and DOJ.

Read more

Lynn Overmann is the executive director at the Beeck Center for Social Impact + Innovation at Georgetown University. Lynn graduated from the NYU School of Law and received her Bachelor of Arts from Bryn Mawr College. A career spanning policy, data, and delivery has prepared her as a leader intent on maximizing social impact.

Lynn’s extensive delivery experience includes serving as a senior advisor for delivery with the U.S. Digital Service, embedded in the Domestic Policy Council, where she advised then-Ambassador Susan Rice on priority policy implementation. She also served as a senior advisor for delivery at the U.S. Department of Agriculture, where she led the department’s work to leverage data and human-centered design to advance equity and improve insights into rural economies.

The strategic use of data to improve people’s daily lives has been a constant driver throughout Lynn’s career. She has served as a senior data strategist at Opportunity Insights, held several senior policy positions at the U.S. Department of Justice where she helped establish the Access to Justice office, and served as deputy chief data officer at the U.S. Department of Commerce. Lynn also founded the Data-Driven Justice Initiative while serving as senior policy advisor to the U.S. Chief Technology Officer in the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy during the Obama administration.

Lynn served as a vice president of criminal justice and head of the D.C. office at Arnold Ventures, leading a team that developed a multimillion-dollar grant portfolio focused on leveraging data and technology to reduce low-level arrests in police departments nationwide. Before this, Lynn was a civil rights and criminal defense attorney in Miami, Florida, spending the first five years of her career as a public defender.

Faith Savaiano

Tech Talent Project State Transition Fellow

Faith is a public policy professional focused on STEM workforce development, technology, and government innovation. She previously served as a Digital Services Expert with the U.S. Digital Service and helped launch a fellowship program placing technical experts into government.

Read more

Faith Savaiano is a public policy professional with expertise in STEM workforce development, technology, and government innovation. She is currently a Tech Talent Project State Transition Fellow and the founder and principal of Savaiano Advisory LLC. Most recently, she served as a Digital Services Expert with the U.S. Digital Service, where she provided policy guidance and contributed to the implementation of workforce and skilling objectives in President Biden’s Executive Order on Artificial Intelligence. Additionally, she served as a subject matter expert on federal regulatory policies and issues, including federal workforce, public-private partnerships, and technology policy. Previously, Faith was the Associate Director, Social Innovation at the Federation of American Scientists, where she helped launch and lead a fellowship program that has now placed 100+ technical experts into government for tours of service. Prior to that time, she worked at a variety of advocacy organizations focused on STEM workforce and education issues and the U.S. Department of State.
